Research

Working papers

  1. Decarbonizing shipping trade: short-term adaptation and long-term abatement strategies

    with Rodica Loisel, Corinne Bagoulla & Pierre Marty

    Earlier version: “Maritime decarbonization pathways: a trade-off between operational and technical measures” — Marcel Boiteux Prize 2024 (FAEE)

    Decarbonizing shipping combines short-term operational levers, such as speed optimization, with the long-term adoption of new technologies and alternative fuels. We build a mixed-integer nonlinear fleet-deployment model calibrated on Asia–Europe liner services to study how carbon pricing shapes both, comparing a global carbon tax with the regionally scoped EU ETS across several technology-maturity scenarios.

    Preliminary results
    • Carbon pricing acts first through speed: vessels slow down, and carriers need additional ships to maintain service frequency. Once low-carbon propulsion becomes competitive, speeds return to their initial level.
    • Fleet size responds non-monotonically to the carbon price, expanding before contracting once low-carbon propulsion becomes competitive.
    • The transition favours larger vessels and progressively redirects cargo toward the most carbon-efficient routes.
    • Without e-fuels, carbon pricing alone delivers only partial emission cuts, and regional pricing through the EU ETS does not trigger structural fuel switching.

    Preliminary results, subject to change.

  2. Global trade under maritime environmental policy

    with Corinne Bagoulla

    The IMO’s sulphur rules — Emission Control Areas and the 2020 global sulphur cap — raise compliance costs for shipping, yet their effect on trade flows remains largely unknown. We build a route-level measure of each trade lane’s exposure to regulated waters and exploit the staggered roll-out of these rules in a structural gravity framework applied to US containerized trade.

    Preliminary results
    • Exposure to sulphur regulation significantly reduces the value of containerized trade on exposed routes.
    • Physical tonnage shows no comparable response: the regulation appears to act on the composition of trade rather than on the volume shipped.
    • The effect builds up after implementation and is absent in placebo tests.

    Preliminary results, subject to change.

  3. Spatial carbon leakage in shipping: port-call evasion under a regional versus a global carbon price

    Single-authored

    Since 2024, the EU ETS prices shipping emissions according to where ships call: in full within the EU, by half on legs entering or leaving it, not at all elsewhere. A carrier can therefore shrink its taxed perimeter by calling at a non-EU hub, without relocating anything. We quantify this leakage with a mobile tax base through a structural model of liner services on the real global port network, and compare the EU ETS with a universal IMO carbon price.

    Preliminary results
    • Most of the abatement gap between the EU ETS and a global price comes from partial coverage, not from rerouting.
    • Port-call evasion is a real but second-order channel, and it grows with the carbon price.
    • Its size hinges on the completeness of the list of neighbouring hubs designated under the anti-evasion rules: a few omissions restore much of the leakage.
    • A transport border carbon adjustment closes the channel, but most of what it gains is carbon that was never priced in the first place.

    Preliminary results, subject to change.
